A wet room replaces the bath or shower tray with a fully waterproofed floor that drains to a single point. A level-access shower works the same way: there is no step or lip, so it is safe and easy to use with reduced mobility, a wheelchair or a shower chair.

## Who are they for?

- **Social housing adaptations** for tenants with changing mobility needs
- **Commercial property**, including staff facilities and accessible washrooms
- **Homeowners** planning ahead, or who simply want a modern, open shower

## What the work involves

1. **Strip-out** of the existing bath, tray, tiles and flooring
2. **Floor preparation** so the floor falls gently towards the drain
3. **Waterproofing (tanking)** of the floor and walls, the most important stage
4. **Non-slip flooring** and wall finishes
5. **Fittings** such as a shower, shower seat, grab rails, basin and WC
6. **Testing and handover**, with photos of the finished room

## Things to plan for

- **Drainage:** the position of the waste pipe affects where the drain can go.
- **Ventilation:** a good extractor fan keeps the room dry.
- **Doors and space:** wider doors and turning space help wheelchair users.
