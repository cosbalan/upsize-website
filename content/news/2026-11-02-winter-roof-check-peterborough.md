Most storm damage we see in Peterborough starts as a small problem that was already there: one slipped tile, a loose ridge or a cracked lead flashing. Winter wind and rain then turn a £200 repair into a ceiling stain, wet insulation and an insurance claim.

If you need a hand, our [roofing team in Peterborough](/roofing-services-peterborough) can check it for you. This checklist shows what you can safely check yourself from the ground and the loft, what each problem usually costs to fix, and when it is worth calling a roofer.

**Stay safe:** never climb onto the roof or lean a ladder against a gutter. Everything below can be checked from the ground, a window or the loft.

## Quick answer: when should you check your roof?

Check it **in October or November**, before the storm season, and again **after any named storm or very high winds**. A small repair done now is cheaper and faster than an emergency repair in January, when roofers are busiest.

## The 8-point winter roof checklist

### 1. Slipped, cracked or missing tiles and slates

Stand back on the pavement or in the garden and look along each slope. Gaps, tiles sitting out of line, or broken pieces in the gutter or on the drive are the usual signs.

**Typical repair:** £170 – £460 for a few tiles.

### 2. Ridge and hip tiles

The ridge is the row of tiles along the top. Look for gaps in the mortar, tiles that look lifted, or bits of mortar on the ground. Mortar-only ridges are the tiles most often blown off in storms.

**Typical repair:** £500 – £1,400 to re-fix or replace a ridge with a mechanical dry-fix system. BS 5534 requires ridges and verges to be mechanically fixed.

### 3. Lead flashings around chimneys and walls

Use binoculars or a zoomed phone photo. Lead should sit flat and tight against the brickwork. Lifted, split or missing lead is a common cause of leaks around chimney breasts.

**Typical repair:** £400 – £1,050 to replace chimney flashings.

### 4. Gutters, downpipes and valleys

Blocked gutters overflow, soak walls and can push water back under the bottom row of tiles. After heavy rain, look for water spilling over the edge, sagging sections or damp patches on the walls below.

See our [gutters, fascias and soffits page](/gutters-fascias-soffits-peterborough) for repairs and replacements.

### 5. Flat roofs on extensions, garages and dormers

Look for standing water a day after rain, blisters, cracks or seams lifting at the edges. Old felt roofs usually fail first in winter, because water freezes and opens up the joints.

If a flat roof keeps needing patches, an [EPDM rubber roof](/flat-roofing-peterborough) is often better value than another repair. Read our [EPDM flat roof guide](/news/epdm-flat-roof-guide-peterborough).

### 6. Inside the loft

This is the most useful check of all. On a dry day, with a torch:

- look for **daylight** coming through the roof;
- look for **damp or dark patches** on the timbers, felt or insulation;
- check around the chimney breast and the water tank;
- smell for damp or mould.

Take photos with the date. They help if you ever need to make an insurance claim.

### 7. Ceilings and top-floor walls

Brown rings, bubbling paint or a musty smell on the top floor usually mean water is already getting in. Don't wait for the next storm. Small leaks spread along timbers before they show.

See [roof leak repair](/roof-damage-repair-peterborough) for how we trace and fix leaks.

### 8. Trees, aerials and satellite dishes

Overhanging branches scrape tiles and fill gutters. Loose aerials and dishes can be pulled off in high winds and damage the tiles around their fixings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a roof be checked?

Once a year is sensible, ideally in autumn, plus after any storm with very high winds. Older roofs and flat roofs benefit from a check every year without fail.

### Can I check my roof myself?

Yes, from the ground, from windows and from inside the loft. Never climb onto the roof or lean a ladder against plastic gutters. Leave anything at height to a roofer with the right access equipment.

### Will my insurance pay for storm damage to my roof?

Usually only for sudden damage caused by a storm, not wear and tear. A roof that has been checked and maintained makes a claim much easier. Read our [storm damage insurance claim guide](/news/roof-storm-damage-insurance-claim-peterborough).
