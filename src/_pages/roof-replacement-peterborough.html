Expert guide

What a proper roof replacement includes

A new roof is a major investment, so it is worth knowing what should be included. Quotes that look cheap often leave out the parts that make a roof last.

  • Full scaffold with edge protection to the eaves, front and back, as HSE guidance requires.
  • Strip to the rafters, with timbers inspected and any rot repaired or replaced.
  • Breathable membrane and new treated battens graded for your tiles.
  • Tiles fixed to BS 5534, with clips and nails where required and extra fixing at the edges.
  • Ventilation at eaves and ridge to prevent condensation in the loft.
  • Dry-fix ridges, hips and verges, and new lead flashings where needed.
  • Waste removed in licensed skips, and the site left clean.

Do you need approval?

Replacing a house roof like-for-like does not usually need planning permission, except for listed buildings or in some conservation areas. Building regulations are different. The Planning Portal says approval is needed for larger re-roofs, structural changes, a covering with different fire performance, or one that increases the roof’s weight by 15% or more. Under Part L, a large re-roof may also need the insulation to be upgraded. We check what applies before we price your job.

How long does a new roof last?

A well-installed concrete tile roof typically lasts 50 years or more, and clay can last longer. The underlay, battens and fixings matter as much as the tiles, which is why we never cut corners on them.

Standards & sources

What the regulations and research say

We work to current British Standards, HSE guidance and Building Regulations. Here are the sources behind the advice on this page.

  • Planning PortalBuilding Regulations: work to an existing roof

    Approval is needed for larger re-roofs, structural alterations, a covering with different fire performance, or one that adds 15% or more to the roof’s weight.

  • GOV.UKApproved Document L: conservation of fuel and power

    When a large part of a roof is renovated, Part L normally requires the insulation to be upgraded where this is technically and economically feasible.

  • MarleyWhat are the fixing requirements of BS 5534?

    Under BS 5534, mortar can no longer be relied on as the only fixing. Single-lap tiles, hips and ridges must be mechanically fixed, and the standard covers re-roofing and repairs.

  • HSERoof work – Health and Safety Executive

    Almost one in five deaths in construction work involve roof work. HSE advises treating every roof as fragile until a competent person confirms otherwise.

FAQs

New roofs & replacement: your questions answered

How much does a new roof cost in Peterborough?

As a guide, a full re-roof with concrete tiles costs around £5,200–£8,600 for a terraced house, £7,500–£12,100 for a 3-bed semi and £9,200–£14,900 for a detached house, including scaffolding and waste removal. Clay tiles and slate cost more.

How long does it take to replace a roof?

Most houses take 3–8 working days, depending on size, access and weather. A terraced house is often 3–5 days and a semi 5–8 days. The roof is kept watertight at the end of every day.

Do I need planning permission to replace my roof?

Not usually, if you are replacing it like-for-like on a house. Listed buildings, some conservation areas and flats can be different, so check with Peterborough City Council or your local council if unsure.

Do I need building regulations approval for a new roof?

Usually, yes, for a full re-roof. Approval is needed for larger re-roofs, structural changes, or a heavier or different covering, and the insulation may need upgrading under Part L. We arrange this as part of the job.

Can you re-roof over the existing tiles?

No. Tiled roofs must be stripped back so the timbers, underlay and battens can be checked and replaced. Laying over old tiles hides problems and adds weight.

Can I stay in my house while the roof is replaced?

Yes. Most people stay at home. The roof is made watertight every evening, and we keep the site and scaffold secure.

What is the best time of year to replace a roof?

Spring to autumn has the most reliable weather, but roofs can be replaced all year with good planning and temporary protection. Booking ahead for summer is wise.

Should I replace my fascias and gutters at the same time?

Usually, yes, if they are old. The scaffold is already up, so doing it together saves a second set of access costs.
