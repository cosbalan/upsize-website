Expert guide

Why storms damage roofs, and how to stop it happening again

Wind does not push tiles off. It sucks them up. As wind flows over a roof, it creates uplift, which is strongest at the edges: the ridge, verges, eaves and corners. Tiles held only by their weight or by old mortar are the first to go.

The fenland and open countryside around Peterborough leave many roofs exposed. During Storm Eunice in February 2022, gusts of 66mph were recorded near Peterborough at RAF Wittering. Across the UK, the ABI estimated around 169,500 property claims from that month’s three storms, costing £473 million.

Repairing to the current standard

The British Standard for slating and tiling, BS 5534, was updated to assume higher wind loads. Single-lap tiles, ridges and hips must now be mechanically fixed, and perimeter tiles need extra fixing. Repairing storm damage to this standard, rather than just bedding tiles back on mortar, is the best way to stop it happening again.

Making a storm damage claim

  • Photograph the damage from the ground and inside, with the date.
  • Tell your insurer as soon as possible. Many have a 24-hour claims line.
  • Arrange an emergency make-safe to prevent further damage, and keep the receipts.
  • Ask your roofer for a written report of the cause and extent of the damage.

The Met Office names a storm when it could cause disruption or damage serious enough for an amber or red warning. A storm warning is a good time to check that your gutters are clear and nothing loose is lying on the roof.

FAQs

Storm & wind damage: your questions answered

Can't see your question? Call 0800 193 9030 or send us a message.

Does home insurance cover storm damage to a roof?

Most buildings insurance covers sudden storm damage, such as tiles blown off in a gale. It does not usually cover wear and tear or damage caused by poor maintenance. Check your policy wording and report the damage as soon as possible.

What wind speed damages roof tiles?

It depends on the roof’s condition and exposure. Well-fixed roofs withstand strong gales, but loose tiles, old mortar-bedded ridges and exposed verges can fail in gusts of 50–60mph or less. Many insurers define a storm by wind speed, so check your policy.

How soon should storm damage be repaired?

As soon as possible. Even one missing tile lets rain reach the underlay and timbers, and loose tiles can fall and injure someone. A quick make-safe limits the damage and supports your claim.

Can I claim for a temporary roof repair?

Usually, yes. Insurers expect you to prevent further damage, and most cover reasonable emergency repairs. Keep receipts and photos and tell your insurer what was done.

Why do ridge tiles blow off in storms?

Older ridges are held only by mortar, which cracks over time. The wind lifts the loose tiles. BS 5534 now requires ridge tiles to be mechanically fixed, and a dry-fix ridge system prevents it happening again.

How can I protect my roof before a storm?

Clear gutters, secure or remove anything loose near the roof, and fix any slipped tiles or cracked ridge mortar before winter. A roof check in autumn is the best prevention.

Will you deal with my insurance company?

We provide the photos, a written damage report and a fixed price, which is what most insurers need. Your claim stays between you and your insurer, but we can speak to their loss adjuster if asked.
