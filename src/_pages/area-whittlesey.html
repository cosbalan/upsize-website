Roofing in Whittlesey

Homes and roofs in Whittlesey

Whittlesey is a Fenland market town east of Peterborough. The centre around the Buttercross is a conservation area with Georgian and Victorian brick houses, many roofed in clay pantiles or Welsh slate, while the outskirts are mostly post-war and modern estates.

What we usually find on roofs here

Open Fen country means strong winds, so we see a lot of slipped pantiles, loose ridge tiles, failed mortar at gable verges and storm-damaged flashings. Chimneys on older terraces often need repointing and new lead.

Local tip from our roofers

Pantiles overlap loosely and rely on good pointing at the verges and ridge. After a windy spell, look along the edges of the roof from the street: a missing or lifted pantile at the verge is the usual start of a leak.

Local roofing notes

Roofing in Whittlesey’s conservation area

Whittlesey is known for its historic mud walls and its long brick-making history, and the older streets still have clay pantile and slate roofs. On these we repair with matching or reclaimed tiles so the roof keeps its character.

Before work on a listed building or in the conservation area, we check with Fenland District Council what consent is needed. Like-for-like repairs elsewhere in town do not normally need planning permission.
