Expert guide

Why lead and chimneys fail, and how we fix them properly

Get my free roofing quoteor call free 0800 193 9030

Chimneys stand above the roof and take the full force of wind and rain, and the joint between stack and roof is a natural weak point. Many “roof leaks” are actually chimney leaks.

Lead done right lasts a lifetime

According to the Lead Sheet Training Academy, correctly installed lead sheet can last over 80 years, and over 100 years for heavier codes. Lead fails early when it is installed wrongly: pieces that are too long split as they expand and contract, so flashings should generally be no longer than 1.5m. We use the right code for each job, keep to the recommended lengths and laps, and chase flashings properly into the brickwork rather than relying on sealant.

Common chimney problems

  • Failed flashings: lead that has lifted, split or been replaced with cement fillets.
  • Eroded mortar joints that let rain soak into the brickwork and show as damp on chimney breasts.
  • Cracked flaunching around the pots, letting water down inside the stack.
  • Open, unused flues that let rain in. Capping with ventilation solves this.
  • Leaning stacks, often from sulphate attack, which may need rebuilding.

Safety first

Chimney work is high-level work. The HSE reports that almost one in five deaths in construction involve roof work, so we always use proper access, either scaffold or a chimney access platform, never just a ladder.

FAQs

Leadwork & chimneys: your questions answered

Can't see your question? Call 0800 193 9030 or send us a message.

How much does it cost to replace chimney flashing?

Replacing the lead flashings around a typical chimney stack usually costs £400–£1,050, plus access if scaffold is needed. The price depends on the stack size and whether repointing is needed at the same time.

How long does lead flashing last?

Correctly installed lead can last over 80 years, and over 100 years for heavier codes, according to the Lead Sheet Training Academy. Early failure is usually due to pieces that are too long, the wrong code or poor fixing.

What code of lead is used for flashings?

Lead sheet comes in different thicknesses, called codes. The right code depends on the job: flashings, valleys, gutters and flat areas each need a suitable thickness. We choose the code for each detail following the Lead Sheet Training Academy’s guidance.

Why is my chimney breast damp?

Common causes are failed flashings, eroded mortar joints, cracked flaunching or an uncapped flue letting rain in. Condensation inside a disused flue can also cause damp. An inspection will find which it is.

Does a leaning chimney need rebuilding?

Often, yes. A noticeable lean is usually caused by sulphate attack on the mortar and can become dangerous in high winds. We assess it and advise whether to rebuild or remove it.

Can I remove a chimney stack?

Removing the stack above the roof is often possible and is then roofed over. Removing chimney breasts inside affects structure and needs building regulations approval, and party wall rules may apply.

Do you need scaffolding to repair a chimney?

In most cases, yes. Safe access is a legal requirement for work at height. Small jobs can sometimes be done from a chimney access platform or roof ladder; we include access in every quote.
